# The brief: what a video must meet to be accepted

Every job works to a brief: a client's clipping brief, a platform's rules, or the channel owner's own wishes. Fill in
this checklist once per job, before picking moments, from whatever the brief says. Where the brief is silent, the
house default in the right-hand column applies. Check every video against the filled-in list before delivery: it
decides whether a video is accepted, and it overrides a house default where the two differ.

## The checklist
| Item | Ask the brief | House default when it says nothing |
|---|---|---|
| Sources allowed | which shows, channels, playlists or episodes may be clipped; which may not (older shows, solo uploads, other people's channels) | only the source the user named; newest episodes first |
| Face in the first seconds | must the speaker's face be on screen early, and by when | the speaker on camera in the first 5 s |
| Subtitles | must subtitles run the whole clip; size and accuracy rules | Shorts: subtitles the whole way (statements count because they show the exact words heard); long form: no subtitles |
| Whole thoughts | must each clip be one complete thought | one whole thought with a payoff; never end mid-sentence or trail into the next topic |
| Meaning | may tangents be dropped | drop a tangent only when the sentences left still say exactly what the speaker meant; never cut a quote so it says something they did not mean |
| Ad reads | are sponsor segments allowed | no ad reads, no sponsor segments, no other brands in frame (no platform logos in graphics either) |
| Outside material | is outside footage allowed; outside images | no outside video; outside images (paintings, documents) only once the user confirms each one |
| Tags and credits | which handles, hashtags and credit lines each platform needs | credit the show and the speaker as the user gives them; no tags you were not given |
| Titles | clickbait rules | the idea of the clip, accurately; no clickbait or misleading titles |
| Audience regions and posting times | where the views must come from, which decides when to post | post when the target audience is awake (a US audience: US daytime) |
| Platform fit | which topics go to which platform | a different cut per platform; never the same clip on two platforms |
| Repost limits | how many similar clips may go up | never post near-identical clips; different ideas in one batch |
| Quality policy | what gets a clip or an account rejected | original, high-retention edits; accurate captions and context; no engagement bait, fake context, recycled clips, bots or stolen edits |
| Engagement and lifetime | minimum engagement, likes visible, how long a post stays up | keep posts up and likes visible unless told otherwise |

## Typical values (examples of what briefs ask for)
Clipping briefs often read like this; copy the real numbers from the brief in front of you, never from here:
- "At least 40 % of views from the US, UK, Canada and Australia" (so post in US daytime).
- "The speaker's face on screen in the first 5 s."
- "Subtitles on the whole clip: big, readable, accurate."
- "Never more than 5 near-identical posts."
- "Tag the show and the host in every caption."
- "No clips from outside the listed links."

## What to clip (shapes that work for any talking head)
| Shape | Looks like |
|---|---|
| The hard truth | one blunt line that reframes a common belief, no build-up |
| The problem everyone has | a situation the viewer recognises, then the answer |
| The story | a short anecdote with a payoff: setup, turn, point |

Scoring and picking are in cutting-formula.md; posting-file format in posting.md.

## Record it
Write the filled-in checklist at the top of the job's `GATES.md` (or in a short note beside the posting files), so
the final report can say, item by item, how each video meets it.
